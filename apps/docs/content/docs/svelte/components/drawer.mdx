title: Drawer

A drawer gives a task its own sheet without leaving the page. It shares [Dialog’s](./dialog) focus and dismissal behavior, then adds dragging, snap points, and a handle you can use with the keyboard.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/basic"
  caption="Open the drawer, adjust the daily goal, then Submit or Cancel. You can also press Escape, press outside, or drag the sheet toward its edge to close it."
/>

## Installation

```package-install
npm install @ui-sdk/svelte
```

## Composition

Use the following composition to build a `Drawer`:

```text
Drawer
├── DrawerTrigger
├── DrawerBackdrop
└── DrawerContent
    ├── DrawerTitle
    ├── DrawerDescription
    └── DrawerClose
```

The content is the sheet. The drawer positions it inside a full-screen viewport and moves it with `translate`. Give it a size, a surface, and corners; leave positioning and translation to the drawer. Give the backdrop a color, such as `bg-black/30`; the drawer manages its opacity.

## Examples

### Scrollable Content

Put long content in an inner `min-h-0 flex-1 overflow-y-auto` area. A downward drag scrolls that area first; when it reaches the top, the same gesture can move the drawer. `initialFocus="content"` starts focus on the sheet itself.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/scrollable"
  caption="Open the long sheet and scroll its text. At the top, drag downward to move the sheet; on a keyboard, Tab reaches the controls and Escape closes it."
/>

### Sides

<span id="rtl" />

`side` chooses bottom, top, left, or right. Logical `start` and `end` follow text direction. The content’s `data-side` tells you which physical edge to style.

Side drawers can opt into `preventEdgeSwipe` to keep an edge gesture from starting iOS Safari’s back or forward navigation. It also prevents clicks right at those edges, so use it only when you need that tradeoff.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/sides"
  caption="Open a sheet from each edge. Drag it back toward that edge, press outside, or press Escape to close it."
/>

### Snap Points

<span id="snap-points-1" />

`snapPoints` are the places the drawer can rest. It opens at the first, or at `defaultSnapPoint`, and settles at another after a drag. A fast fling can skip intermediate points.

| Value              | Size                                       |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| `0.5`              | Half the viewport along the drawer’s axis. |
| `320` or `"320px"` | Pixels; numbers above 1 are pixel values.  |
| `"20rem"`          | A multiple of the root font size.          |
| `"50%"`            | A percentage of the viewport.              |
| `"content"`        | The sheet’s own size, the default.         |

The sheet’s CSS size is the limit: `1` cannot make a short sheet fill the screen. Use a height such as `h-[calc(100dvh-3rem)]` when the largest point should nearly fill it. The drawer measures again when content or available space changes; a moved resting point reports `content-resize`.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/snap-points"
  caption="Open the sheet at its middle snap point. Drag or press the handle to try the smaller and larger resting points."
/>

### Handle

A Handle is a button with an accessible name. Pressing it moves to the next larger snap point, then wraps to the smallest. Arrow keys along the drawer’s axis move one point without wrapping; Home and End move to the ends. `canExpand` and `canCollapse` let the example show which moves remain.

For a sheet with one snap point, use a decorative bar instead of an inactive button.

### Controlled

Use `bind:open` and `bind:snapPoint`. You can control opening and snap points independently. Cancel a proposed change with `details.cancel()`; a canceled swipe springs back. `onOpenChangeComplete` runs after the sheet settles.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/controlled"
  caption="Toggle open, choose a snap point, or move the sheet itself. The readout and log show the state and the reason for each change."
/>

### Not Dismissible

With `dismissible` false, Escape, outside presses, back gestures, and swipes leave the drawer open. `onDismissAttempt` tells you what was tried, so you can offer a way to discard unsaved work. Close parts, triggers, and controllers can still close it deliberately.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/non-dismissible"
  caption="Open the draft, then try Escape or a swipe to close it. The sheet stays open and reports the attempt; Discard or Post closes it explicitly."
/>

### Modal Above a Snap Point

<span id="modality-per-snap-point" />

Set `modalAbove` when the page should stay usable beside a small sheet. At or below that point there is no dim, inert page, scroll lock, or `aria-modal`. Above it, `modal` applies. The switch happens when the drawer settles and does not move focus; `data-interactive` exposes the usable-page state.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/non-modal"
  caption="Open directions at the small snap point and choose another place behind the sheet. Expand it to make the sheet modal, then collapse it to use the page again."
/>

### Modal

`modal` has the same choices as [Dialog](./dialog#modal): `true` blocks the page, `"trap-focus"` keeps Tab inside while leaving the page usable, and `false` allows focus to leave. A non-modal drawer closes on an outside press only when `closeOnOutsidePress` is enabled.

### Nested Drawers

<span id="nesting-and-stacking" />

A drawer opened inside another joins its stack. Escape and outside presses affect the topmost one; closing a parent closes its children. `--drawer-stack` follows the sheets above it as they open and drag, so a parent can scale back with `[scale:calc(1-var(--drawer-stack)*0.04)]`.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/nested"
  caption="Open Account, then Change password. The parent steps back as the child opens; Escape closes one sheet at a time."
/>

### Forms

A Close part’s `action` can return a Promise. The sheet stays open while it runs, including through Escape or a swipe. A rejection leaves the action’s error state available for a retry. See [Dialog’s async actions](./dialog#forms) for the shared pending and error contract.

This example also uses `keepMounted` to preserve unfinished fields and `finalFocus` to return to the updated profile.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/form"

/>

### Outlets

An Outlet makes another element follow the sheet’s progress. Its `keyframes` run from collapsed to fully expanded; this example scales and rounds the inbox behind a compose sheet. An outlet outside the drawer connects through its controller.

<ComponentExample

### Opening from Anywhere

Connect external triggers and the drawer to one `createDrawerController<Payload, Result>()`. Each trigger passes a `payload`; the content reads it from its state. This lets one sheet serve every row, including disabled triggers for unavailable actions.

Calling `open({ payload })` on the controller returns a Promise with the closing `reason` and `returnValue`. `getSnapPoint()` and `setSnapPoint()` also let code read or move the sheet. The [controller guide](../controllers) covers the shared result and payload flow.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/controller"
  caption="Share a file from its row or use Share the roadmap. Choose Copy link or Send by email to return a result; the uploading file’s trigger is disabled."
/>

### Action Sheet

A sheet can offer choices instead of a form. Give each Close part a `value`; the open Promise returns the chosen action. Escape, an outside press, or a swipe returns no value, which this example treats as Cancel.

Put the least risky action first because a keyboard or code open starts there. Use an [Alert Dialog](./alert-dialog) when a choice needs confirmation.

<ComponentExample
  name="drawer/action-sheet"
  caption="Open Photo options and choose an action. The readout shows the returned value; dismissing the sheet counts as Cancel."

### Native Scrolling

<span id="drivers" />

`driver="scroll-snap"` uses native scrolling and CSS snap points for a bottom sheet. `"pointer"` uses pointer dragging and spring motion on every side.

The default `"auto"` chooses native scrolling for a bottom sheet on a touch screen. It uses pointer dragging for mouse and pen, other sides, `modalAbove`, or `expandOnScroll` false. `data-driver` reports the active choice. Both drivers use the same snap points and state.

### Theme Color

`themeColor` dims an existing `<meta name="theme-color">` with the backdrop. It leaves pages without that tag alone. This demo supplies a neutral tag when needed so you can see the change in its readout.

### Physics

<span id="physics-1" />

Override part of `sheetPhysics.ios` with `physics`. `response` changes spring timing, `dampingRatio` changes settling, `momentumDampingRatio` affects flings, and `rubberBand` changes resistance past the largest point. These options affect the pointer driver, so the example selects it explicitly.

### Persistent Elements

`persistentElements` keeps selected elements usable while the drawer is modal. Pass an array of elements or selectors, or a function returning elements. Something that must stay above the backdrop also needs its own top-layer surface, as the chat button has here.

### Container

Drawers normally render in place and use the top layer. `container` moves the backdrop and viewport elsewhere, for example into a shadow root or out of a surrounding form. See the shared [popup guide](../popups).

### Responsive

This recipe uses a media query to choose a Drawer on a narrow screen and a Dialog on a wide screen. It replaces the component tree at the breakpoint, so an open sheet closes and local field edits reset. Lift the open and field values above that branch if they must survive the switch.

## Keyboard and Focus

The drawer shares [Dialog’s focus behavior](./dialog#focus): focus moves inside on open and returns when it closes. A touch open focuses the content so a field does not summon the keyboard immediately. When the on-screen keyboard opens, the viewport shrinks and the focused field can scroll into view inside the sheet.

## Styling

<DataAttributes component="drawer" />

Leave the content’s `translate` and the backdrop’s opacity to the drawer. You can style scale, corners, colors, and the inner scroll area. A bottom sheet can use an `after` element below its edge to cover the gap during overscroll, as the examples do.

The drawer respects reduced motion, fading instead of sliding.

### Controller

Create one with `createDrawerController<Payload, Result>()` and pass it as `controller`, to the drawer and to any
triggers and outlets outside it.

Every write method takes an optional `{ reason, event }`, passed to the callbacks. Closing with a controller always works, even when the drawer isn't dismissible or an action is running.

### Events

Callbacks get the new value first, then the details. A bound `open` or `snapPoint` only updates when its callback wasn't canceled.

| Prop                   | On            | Receives                                                                             |
| ---------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `bind:open`            | `Drawer`      | Whether the drawer is open                                                           |
| `bind:snapPoint`       | `Drawer`      | The snap point it rests on                                                           |
| `onOpenChange`         | `Drawer`      | `(open, details)`: the new state, the [reason](#reasons) and any `returnValue`       |
| `onOpenChangeComplete` | `Drawer`      | `(open)`, once it has settled                                                        |
| `onPayloadChange`      | `Drawer`      | `(payload, details)`: the content's new payload, and the [reason](#reasons)          |
| `onSnapPointChange`    | `Drawer`      | `(snapPoint, details)`: the new snap point and the [reason](#reasons)                |
| `onDismissAttempt`     | `Drawer`      | `({ reason, event })`, when a dismissal was refused                                  |
| `action`               | `DrawerClose` | `(event, { source })`: the action to run. Its returned Promise keeps the drawer open |

## Accessibility

The trigger is a button that opens a dialog, and the drawer follows the [WAI-ARIA modal dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/) while it's modal: `aria-modal`, focus trapped inside, the page inert, labelled by its title and described by its description. Escape closes it. The handle is a button that controls the drawer: a press moves to the next snap point, arrow keys along the drawer's axis step, and Home and End go to the ends.
